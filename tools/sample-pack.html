Touches 2 and 3 follow the same shape with a different reason and a harder call to action (16 more pieces in the full kit). Every piece names the sender, the practice, and an opt-out.

Why “counts, not names” is not a marketing line. You send from your own phone or PMS. We receive segment counts, then results — contacted, replied, booked, attended, production. No name, birth date or insurance ID ever enters an email, a spreadsheet or a chat with us.

What the numbers say (sample): group A carried the response (11.3% booked); group C booked rarely but produced the highest value per visit. Calls converted 9 of the 34 bookings — the script did more work than the third touch.

  • Next month: send group C a second letter with a different reason (medication and gum health), and call every B that replied but did not book.
  • Front desk: keep the call window at 9:30–11:00am — afternoons went unanswered in this sample.
  • Care plan: birthday message + insurance-max reminder, both already written.
The guarantee, as it appears in the sprint: if the three planned sends are completed and fewer than 5 patients rebook, the next month of Recall Care is free. Patient numbers are never promised — the promise covers the work: segments, copy, calendar, tracker, three check-ins.
Compliance note. Messages go to existing patients only, through the practice’s own records and consent, and every piece includes an opt-out line. The practice keeps the patient list and the record of each send. If a practice asks us to send on its behalf, that requires a signed BAA and HIPAA-compliant tooling — a separate, quoted scope.
